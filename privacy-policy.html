Target Drop – Privacy Policy

Effective date: October 8, 2026 · Last updated: November 9, 2026

This Privacy Policy explains what information is handled when you play Target Drop, a mobile game for Android, and what choices you have. Target Drop is developed and published by Abdellah Moukhless (“Moukhless”, “we”, “us”), an independent developer based in Morocco.

In short

  • You do not need an account, and the game never asks for your name, email address, phone number, photos, contacts or precise location.
  • Your progress and settings are saved on your device. We do not run servers of our own.
  • The game shows ads from Google AdMob. To show and measure ads and prevent fraud, Google collects some information from your device, such as your IP address, the advertising ID and how you interact with ads.
  • In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, you are asked for consent before personalised ads are shown, and you can change your choice at any time in the game’s settings.
  • In US states with a consumer privacy law, such as California, you can opt out of the “sale” or “sharing” of your personal information for ads at any time with Privacy options in the game’s settings.
  • To see how far players get and to fix crashes, the game sends gameplay statistics (for example runs, scores and ranks reached) and crash reports to Google Firebase. They are linked to random IDs, never to your name. To see how much the ads earn per player, these statistics are also connected to our Google AdMob account.
  • Target Drop sells nothing: there are no in-app purchases.

1. Information stored on your device

To keep your progress, the game saves the following on your device only (in the app’s private storage):

  • game progress: coins, best scores, rank and experience, unlocked skins, boosts, daily missions and challenges;
  • settings: language, sound, music and vibration, and whether the tutorial has been completed.

Your saved progress itself is not uploaded to us or to anyone else, and it cannot identify you. Separately, the game sends some gameplay statistics and crash reports to Google Firebase, as described in section 3.

2. Advertising (Google AdMob)

Target Drop is free and is funded by ads: rewarded ads that you choose to watch (for example to continue a run or to get free coins) and an occasional ad between games. Ads are provided by Google AdMob through the Google Mobile Ads SDK. According to Google, the SDK collects and shares the following data for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention:

DataExamples
Approximate locationYour IP address, used to estimate your general location (country or city), never your precise GPS location.
Device identifiersThe Android advertising ID and the app set ID.
InteractionsApp launches, taps on ads and ad video views.
DiagnosticsApp and SDK performance information, such as launch time, freezes and energy use.

This data is collected and processed by Google, not by us; we only see summary reports (for example how many ads were shown and how much they earned). Google’s use of this data is described in the Google Privacy Policy and in How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services.

Your choices about ads

  • Consent (EEA, UK and Switzerland): the first time you open the game, Google’s consent form asks whether ads may be personalised. If you refuse, you may still see ads, but they are not personalised. You can change your choice at any time with Privacy options in the game’s Settings.
  • Opt-out (US states): if you live in a US state with a consumer privacy law (for example California, Colorado, Connecticut, Virginia or Texas), the ad data that the Google Mobile Ads SDK collects in the game (see above) may count as a “sale” or “sharing” of personal information, or as targeted advertising, under that law. You can opt out at any time: open Privacy options in the game’s Settings and choose Don’t sell or share my data. Google then limits how it uses that data, and the ads you still see are no longer personalised with it.
  • Advertising ID: on Android you can reset or delete your advertising ID in your device settings (usually Settings › Privacy › Ads or Settings › Google › Ads). When it is deleted, apps can no longer use it for personalised ads.
  • Google ad settings: you can control how Google personalises ads at My Ad Center.

3. Game statistics and crash reports (Google Firebase Analytics and Crashlytics)

To understand how far players get in the game and to find and fix bugs, Target Drop uses Google Analytics for Firebase and Firebase Crashlytics, two services provided by Google. For these services Google acts as our data processor (service provider) and handles the data on our behalf. The game sends:

DataWhat is sent
Gameplay eventsWhen a run starts and ends (game mode, score, time played, best score, whether it is a new best, number of continues and run number), when you reach a new rank or a new challenge level in a mode, and how far you get in the tutorial (step reached, finished or skipped).
Standard app eventsFirst launch, app updates, sessions, screens viewed and time spent in the game, which the Firebase SDK records automatically.
IdentifiersA random app-instance ID and a Firebase installation ID created by the Firebase SDK and, where allowed, the Android advertising ID.
Device and approximate locationDevice model, Android version, app version and language, and your country, region or city, estimated from a shortened (masked) IP address. Never your precise GPS location.
Ad earningsFor each ad shown in the game: the ad format and the money it earned, reported by Google AdMob.
Crash reportsIf the game crashes or hits a serious error: the error details (stack trace), the state of the app and device at that moment (for example device model, Android version, free memory and screen orientation), a random Crashlytics installation ID and the last game events before the crash.

We use this data only in summary reports, for example how many players finish the tutorial or reach rank 5, which game mode is played most, how much the ads earn per player, and which crashes happen on which devices. We do not use it to identify you and we do not combine it with other information about you. Firebase is linked to our Google AdMob account (the ads described in section 2): Google Analytics receives the earnings of each ad shown in the game, and AdMob can use the game statistics in its reports. Google signals is turned off, the statistics are not linked to Google Ads, and sharing with Google for its own products is turned off. The game never sends your name, your email address or anything you type.

Consent: in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, the game turns off Firebase Analytics’ identifiers and advertising features as soon as it detects that these consent rules apply (within the first seconds of the first launch) and keeps them off; only limited data without identifiers may be sent after that. The events of those first seconds, such as the first app open, may already have been sent. In other countries, game statistics are sent while you play. Crash reports are sent in every country, because we need them to keep the game working.

Your choices: there is no in-game switch for these statistics. You can get new random IDs at any time by clearing the game’s data (this also deletes your progress), and you can stop all collection by uninstalling the game. More information: Privacy and Security in Firebase and the Google Privacy Policy.

4. Permissions

The game uses only the Android permissions needed to work and to show ads: internet and network state (to load ads), the advertising ID permission (used by the Google Mobile Ads SDK as described above) and vibration (for haptic feedback, which you can turn off in Settings). It does not use your camera, microphone, contacts, files, photos or precise location.

The Google Mobile Ads SDK also adds a few technical permissions of its own: Android’s ad services permissions (advertising ID, ad attribution and topics), which let it use the privacy features built into Android for showing and measuring ads, and the wake lock and foreground service permissions, which a background-task library included with the SDK uses to finish short tasks such as sending ad reports. The game’s own code does not use these permissions.

Firebase Analytics also uses Google Play’s install referrer service, which tells it whether the game was installed from the Play Store or from an ad.

5. How information is protected

Your game data stays in the app’s private storage, which other apps cannot read. Google states that the data collected by its Mobile Ads SDK and by Firebase is encrypted in transit using TLS. We do not store any personal information on servers of our own.

6. Retention and deletion

  • Game data: it stays on your device until you uninstall Target Drop or clear its data (Settings › Apps › Target Drop › Storage › Clear data). Both delete it permanently. We cannot restore it, because we never receive a copy.
  • Advertising data: it is kept by Google according to Google’s retention policy. You can reset or delete your advertising ID at any time as described above.
  • Game statistics and crash reports: in Google Analytics, detailed event data is deleted after 2 months, and data linked to your random app-instance ID is deleted 14 months after you last play; summary reports without identifiers can be kept longer. Crashlytics deletes crash reports after 90 days.

7. Children

Target Drop is intended for a general audience and is not directed at children under 13 (or the minimum age required in your country). We do not knowingly allow the collection of personal information from children. If you believe a child has provided personal information through the game, contact us and we will help remove it.

8. Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or some US states), you may have the right to access, correct or delete personal data about you, to object to or restrict its use, to withdraw consent, and to complain to a data protection authority. We hold no information that names you: game statistics and crash reports are linked only to random IDs, so we cannot find yours from your name or email address. Clearing the game’s data or uninstalling it gives you new IDs, and the old data is deleted automatically as described in section 6. Requests about advertising data are best made to Google, and consent for personalised ads can be changed in the game at any time. In US states with a consumer privacy law, your rights can also include opting out of the sale or sharing of your personal information and of targeted advertising, which you can do with Privacy options in the game’s Settings. Using any of these rights never changes how the game works for you. If you need help, write to us and we will answer within 30 days.

9. Changes to this policy

We may update this policy, for example when the game gains new features. The new version will be published on this page with a new “Last updated” date. If a change affects how your information is used, we will also mention it in the game’s store listing or in the game.
